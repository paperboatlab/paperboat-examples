# Report: grey-box identification and MPC on bench 3's Furuta pendulum

**Bench 3 · 16–25 November 2026**

## Summary

The pendulum's controller was designed from the kit's datasheet. On a fast arm move (60° out and
back, about 0.7 s each way) it held the pendulum, but only just: the pendulum ran 13.0° off its plan,
the arm overshot to 85.6° (the cables allow 90°), and the amplifier saturated.

We fitted six of the model's physical parameters to an excitation run (grey-box, equation error).
The motor turned out 20 % weaker than rated, the pendulum's centre of mass 7 mm further out, and the
arm's friction nearly twice the datasheet's, with some Coulomb. The identified model replays a
validation run at 94 % (arm) and 91 % (pendulum); the datasheet's at 33 % and −59 %.

On the identified model, an MPC kept the pendulum within **2.0°** of its plan, **84 % less** than the
datasheet LQR: **H1 holds** (the bar was 60 %). But the LQR redesigned on the same model did better
(1.6°, and half the MPC's arm error). **The identification did the work; MPC added nothing on this
move**: nothing constrained the loop (the MPC's ±4 V never became active), and the block's solver
limited its horizon to 0.4 s.

## The rig and the question

A DC motor turns a horizontal arm, a free pendulum hangs from its tip ([[rig-datasheet]]). The
loop runs at 500 Hz on 8192-count encoders through a ±10 V amplifier. The question ([[hypothesis]]):
does an MPC on an identified model hold the pendulum within 40 % of the datasheet LQR's worst
deviation from its plan, with the arm within ±90° and the voltage within ±10 V?

Every closed-loop run followed the same plan ([[protocol-balance-move]]): Julix planned the move on the
model's flat output, so the plan carries the whole state and the feedforward voltage; the loop only
corrects around it.

## Discussion

- **Where the datasheet went wrong.** The LQR gain barely changed with the identified model (at most
  16 % on any term), but the plan did: a feedforward from the datasheet's motor and pendulum pushes
  the rig's elsewhere, and the loop spends its effort fighting its own plan. Planning on the model
  makes a model error count twice.
- **Why MPC didn't pay.** The block condenses the horizon into one dense QP with no terminal cost.
  Over 1 s (N = 200) it would match the LQR, but on an unstable plant that QP is too badly scaled for
  the block's solver, and the loop diverged; N = 80 (0.4 s) is the longest that works, and it leaves
  the arm loose. Add the one-step latency, and its strength, bounds on the input, had nothing to bound:
  the correction peaked at 2.3 V.
- **Caveats.** One run per experiment. The flows were checked in Simplicial against the station
  (E4 1.47°, E5 1.85°, E1 8.61°, without the bench's 2 ms delay), except the estimate flow, whose nlp
  block needs a toolkit built with Ipopt. $k_m$ is probably biased low by the amplifier's dead band
  ([[issue-km-bias]]). The arm hunts 2.4–4° at the holds in every run ([[issue-arm-hunting]]).
